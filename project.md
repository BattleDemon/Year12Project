Task 2: Year 12 Project Report (T/A)
Length: 2000 words+ (T) or 1400 words+ (A)
Weighting: 35%
Task Overview
The Year 12 Project Report presents the research foundation for your creative product and
documents the development of your project.
In this report, you demonstrate your understanding of your topic through research, explain
and justify your creative decisions, and show how your ideas were developed, refined, and
realised in your final product. You also reflect on your learning, evaluating your process,
challenges, and the effectiveness of your final product.
Report Structure
• Page 1 (Cover Page): Report Title by Student Name
• Page 2: Contents Page with all report headings and page numbers
• Pages 3 onwards:
1. Introduction
● Introduce your topic I.e. the focus of your research
● State your line of enquiry
● Introduce your product and its purpose
● Signal what you will cover in the report
Example Introduction
This report investigates the decline of ocean biodiversity, with a particular focus on the
impact of human activity on marine ecosystems. The guiding line of enquiry is: How can
visual storytelling raise awareness of ocean conservation among young audiences? The final
product developed for this project is an illustrated children’s picture book that follows the
journey of a migrating whale and the challenges it faces as a result of pollution and climate
change. The product is intended to communicate complex environmental issues in an
accessible and engaging way for a younger audience. This report outlines the research
undertaken, examines how key ideas informed the development of the product, and
documents the process of planning and creating the final piece.
2. Research
Present the key information gathered from your research. If you carried out original research
such as a survey or other form of data collection, then your findings will be included in this
section.
Organise this section using subheadings based on different areas of your investigation.

● Whale Migration
● The Impact of Human Activity on Marine Ecosystems

Within each subheading:
● introduce the area of research including key concepts
● integrate and cite evidence and make any relevant links between sources
● integrate your original research findings such as data you collected (if relevant)
● integrate and cite visual evidence, such as photos or diagrams, as needed
● discuss how the research discoveries may be applicable to your project
Use a formal, third-person tone in this section unless it is necessary to use the first person.
3. Product Rationale
Explain and justify your product as a whole. You may organise your discussion into
subheadings of your choice. You should address:
● what your product is and why you chose this form
● what your product is designed to achieve (purpose)
● who the product is for (audience)
● how your research informed your decisions
● specific creative decisions you made and the rationale for those decisions
● how the product communicates ideas (language, structure, techniques)
● how the product serves a need or fills a gap
● what value it provides to a community or audience
Analyse specific examples from your work to support your response. You may use the first
person in this section; however, your tone should remain academic.
4. Process (Development and Production)
Discuss how your product developed from initial idea through to final execution. You may
organise your discussion into subheadings of your choice. Include:
● the steps you took (planning, drafting, refining)
● how your ideas changed over time
● evidence of your process (photos, drafts, notes, feedback)
You must also:
● explain any challenges you encountered
● explain how you solved problems
● explain how you improved your product
3
This section should clearly show your thinking, decision-making, and progress. It is
appropriate to write this section using the first person.
5. Reflection
Reflect on your learning and development. You should:
● explain what you learned through the project
● describe how your understanding developed
● discuss what worked well
● identify what you would improve
● give specific examples
● evaluate how effectively your product met its purpose
You must use the first person in this section.
6. Conclusion
● summarise the key outcomes of your investigation
● reinforce how research informed your product
● briefly reflect on the overall success of the project
Reference List
● include all sources
● use Harvard referencing
Appendix
Note: You may integrate selected images, diagrams, or brief extracts within the body of your
report where they directly support your analysis. However, any material that is longer than
approximately 1–1.5 pages, or would disrupt the flow of your writing, should be placed in the
appendix and referenced clearly in the report.
How to format your appendix:
• Place the appendix after your reference list
• Label each item clearly (e.g. Appendix A, Appendix B, Appendix C)
• Give each appendix a title
• Example: Appendix A: First Draft of Picture Book Illustrations
• Present each appendix item on a separate page where possible
Referencing your Appendix in the report:
You must refer to your appendix within your report when relevant.
Examples:
“This stage of development is shown in Appendix A.”
“Feedback from peers (see Appendix B) influenced the revision of…”

Formal Language Tips
Use clear, precise, and formal language throughout your report. Your writing should sound
objective, controlled, and appropriate for an academic audience.
Avoid informal or vague language. Replace it with more precise alternatives:
“big issue” → significant issue / major concern
“a lot of” → many / a range of / numerous
“things” → specific terms (e.g. factors, ideas, elements, issues, processes)
“shows” → demonstrates / indicates / illustrates / reveals
“gets” → obtains / develops / achieves / receives
“makes” → creates / produces / contributes to / results in
“stuff” → materials / content / evidence
“really important” → highly significant / critical / essential
“good” → effective / successful / appropriate
“bad” → ineffective / limited / problematic
Avoid conversational or casual phrasing:
“you can see that…” → “This demonstrates that…”
“this is about…” → “This focuses on…”
“it talks about…” → “It explores…” or “It examines…”
“I think…” (in analysis sections) → “This suggests that…” or “This indicates that…”
Avoid contractions:
don’t → do not
can’t → cannot
won’t → will not
it’s → it is
Instead of general words, aim for accurate terminology related to your topic
Example: “how people act” → “human behaviour”
Example: “how things change” → “how conditions evolve”
