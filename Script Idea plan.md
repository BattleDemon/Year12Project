Introduction and Opening
Est time 1 min 30 sec (0:00 -> 1:30)

- Begin with an engaging question or scenario about changing history through video games.
    
- Introduce grand strategy games and the concept of historical simulation.
    
- Introduce your project, its title, and what you ultimately created.
    
- Briefly establish your guiding line of enquiry.

Origins and Initial ambitions
est time 2 mins 30 sec (1:30 -> 4:00)

- Explain your interest in history, gaming and software development.
    
- Discuss why you decided to combine these interests into a project.
    
- Explain your original ambition of developing a playable grand strategy game.
    
- Introduce the initial scope and the ideas that influenced it.
    
- Establish what you originally hoped to achieve compared with what was realistically possible.

Research
est time 4 mins (4:00 -> 8:00)

- Explain what a grand strategy game actually is, assuming most of the audience is unfamiliar with the genre.
    
- Introduce the importance of interconnected political, military, diplomatic and economic systems.
    
- Discuss the three central areas of your research: historical accuracy and authenticity, player agency, and strategic depth.
    
- Use examples from existing games to explain these concepts.
    
- Link your research to the direction of your own game design.

Development and Evolution
est time 4 mins 30 seconds (8:00 -> 12:30)

- Explain your initial plans for building the game, including the intention to use Godot.
    
- Discuss the realisation that the original scope was too large.
    
- Describe the transition from a full game to a limited prototype, and eventually to a Game Design Document.
    
- Explain how the historical and geographical scope was reduced.
    
- Discuss the challenges of managing time, the scale of development, and deciding what was achievable.
    
- Reflect on how these decisions changed your understanding of game development.

Final Product
est time 2 mins 30 seconds (12:30 -> 15:00)

- Introduce the GDD, explaining what it is and its purpose.
    
- Explain the historical setting, focusing on the Franks, the late Roman world and the emergence of the Merovingian kingdom.
    
- Discuss the intended gameplay experience and the player's role.
    
- Explore the main proposed mechanics, particularly characters, populations, warfare and their interaction.
    
- Explain how research into existing games influenced your design choices.
    
- Discuss how the document balances historical representation, player freedom and gameplay complexity.
    
- Explain how the GDD could support future development.

Evaluation and Reflection 
est time 3 mins 30 sec (15:00 -> 18:30)

- Evaluate the final product against your original objectives.
    
- Discuss the strengths and limitations of producing a design document rather than a playable game.
    
- Reflect on the most important challenges and what you learnt from them.
    
- Consider how your understanding of game design and project management changed.
    
- Discuss the potential significance of the project, both personally and for people interested in historical games.

Conclusion
Est time 1 min 30 sec (18:30 -> 20:00)

- Summarise how the project developed from its original ambitions.
    
- Return to your guiding line of enquiry and what the project taught you about balancing history and gameplay.
    
- Discuss the possibility of developing the game further in the future.
    
- Finish with a reflection on the value of the project beyond the completed document.
